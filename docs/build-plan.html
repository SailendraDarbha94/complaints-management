The design principle is one column: waiting_on. Every open case is always parked against exactly one party — the officer, the complainant, a respondent, the committee, or GDCRI — and the home screen is that list, grouped by who you are chasing, sorted by how late they are. Everything else in the product exists to keep that column honest.

Why this and not the obvious thing

The seven design studies behind this plan collectively specified about sixty tables, four hash chains, two PDF engines, a template IDE, a native member app with screenshot detection, and an AI subsystem — roughly a year of solo part-time work with nothing usable until the end. That is cut. Phase 1 is nineteen tables, eight states, one screen, and a daily digest. It is useful alone, and it fixes the stated problem.

Dates are events, not columns

Each date lives in an append-only case_milestone row, so adding a register column later is a view change rather than a migration. Every milestone carries a mandatory date_source: recorded, from_physical_register, or estimated_by_officer. Anything other than recorded is footnoted in every export and printed case file — which is what stops a reconstructed backlog date from becoming indistinguishable from a recorded fact in an RTI reply or a writ petition.

Two numbering series, and only one of them is ours

The software owns the complaint serial (KSDC/COMP/2026-27/0042) and the RTI serial. It never generates an outward despatch number — that book is shared with certificates and circulars issued by people who will never touch this software, and minting numbers would put our 298 against a clerk's handwritten 298. Until a letter is stamped, the despatch field prints as KSDC/____/2026-27, a pen-fillable blank, and a follow-up chases you to type the real number in afterwards. The complaint number always prints on its own reference line under the subject; that line never blanks.

Case lifecycle

Eight states in v1, and waiting_on is derived from the state by the database rather than stored — so it cannot drift, and a dashboard that looks authoritative can never be quietly wrong.

Sub judice, a party indisposed, or awaiting an external authority are handled by an on_hold flag with a reason — not by extra states. It suppresses deadlines and shows in its own dashboard bucket.

The rule that protects a named dentist

The notice counter increments only when you confirm a letter actually went out. A timer never touches it. Escalation reminders are titled “no reply logged — check the mailbox”, never “did not respond”, because in Phase 1 the software genuinely cannot know the difference — and the premise of this project is that things get forgotten. Generating notice 2 or 3 shows you the full correspondence log first and asks you to confirm. Ex parte eligibility is computed from real despatched notices with proof of service, never from a reminder counter.

Closure is yours

You close a case, with a reason. Committee cover is available and optional, never a gate — a sitting is six to eight weeks away and a closure cannot wait for it. Reopening writes a row with a mandatory reason and returns the case to scrutiny.

On appeals: this design lets you reopen a closed case with a recorded reason — say the patient writes back disputing the order, or a court directs a rehearing. It does not model a formal appeal to the NDC as a separate proceeding, because we do not know whether the council receives those. If it does, that becomes a small addition rather than a redesign.

04

The decisions that shaped it

Each of these was contested during design. The rejected alternative is recorded so the argument is not re-run in three months.

D1

v1 is the follow-up engine and nothing else

Nineteen tables, eight states, one dashboard, plain-text letters, a register export. Committee, GDC letters, RTI, the dentist registry, AI and the member app are later phases — named and dated, not silently dropped.

RejectedBuilding the full sixty-table design and shipping once, nine to fifteen months from now.

D2

The software never mints an outward despatch number

Those fields are blank until you type in what the office stamped, with a follow-up chasing you to do it. The outward register is a physical book shared across the whole council.

RejectedAllocating the office-wide serial automatically — which would corrupt the one document the council already produces in court.

D3

Three notices → ex parte is a warning, not a hard block

With a typed-reason override, plus fields for the service mode and a scanned acknowledgement-due card. Software that refuses to record what the committee actually decided gets worked around.

RejectedHard-coding the rule as a guard — it came from one sentence in an interview, not from a statute anyone has checked.

D4

No native member app in v1 — despite your answer to Q25

Members get the per-sitting case bundle PDF (exactly the printouts they receive today, generated instead of photocopied) plus a read-only mobile-web case sheet behind a one-time-code link, with every document view logged. Usage is instrumented. After three sittings, two or more active members is the recorded go/no-go for building the Expo app.

RejectedMonths of watermarking, screenshot detection and offline bundles resting on three honorary dentists changing their habits — an assumption we can test for free first.

D5

No in-app voting in v1 — despite your answer to Q26

A sitting produces one decision row: date, operative text, attendance, whether it was unanimous / majority / consensus, and optional dissent notes, plus a separate outcome per respondent. Quorum is displayed as a warning, never a block.

RejectedVote cards, tallies, casting votes and quorum enforcement. A hard block would refuse to record a decision actually taken after the patient and dentist had travelled to Bengaluru.

D6

Neither the Registrar nor the Chairperson can block anything

“Awaiting the Registrar's signature” is not a case state; it is a task on your list titled “Get the Registrar to sign…”. Decision letters unlock per case the moment that case's decision is recorded, never behind sitting-wide minutes.

RejectedGating the pipeline on a tap from someone who, by your own answer, does not log in.

D7

Drop Vercel — deploy Next.js to Cloud Run too

Vercel is a USD invoice from a US entity for a council whose procurement has not started. Putting the web app next to the API gives one vendor, one INR invoice, one region, one pipeline.

RejectedVercel Pro at about ₹1,760/month plus a maintained migration path — the maintained escape hatch was itself an admission the choice would be reversed.

D8

Configuration is one row of JSON and a seed file, not fifteen tables

Deadlines, the notice ladder, working days, holidays, outcome lists and closure reasons live in one validated document with no settings screens in v1. Onboarding council number two is a second seed file.

RejectedFifteen configuration tables each implying a CRUD screen — four to six weeks of work for a single tenant with a single set of values.

D9

Multi-tenancy is in the schema from day one; multi-tenant features are not

A council_id on every table, enforced by the database itself rather than by application code, plus a test that fails the build if council B can see one row of council A. The council picker works from day one even with one council. That part is genuinely irreversible; editable-by-UI settings are not.

D10

AI ships switched off

No API key is provisioned. Every intake and drafting form works fully manually first. The gate to turn it on is a signed one-page note to the Registrar stating plainly what leaves India.

RejectedShipping AI-assisted intake in v1 with the disclosure deferred. At two to four complaints a month, typing one in by hand takes ten minutes.

Phase 1

The follow-up engine

Fix “I keep forgetting to reach out”. Nothing else. Nineteen tables with database-level tenant isolation and the audit chain; the eight-state lifecycle with one test per transition; the follow-up engine where escalation creates a new row and snoozing can never launder an overdue case into a clean one; the Today screen; a 09:00 IST digest to your own address; manual case intake with documents; plain-text templates with a copy panel and an “I have sent this” button that starts the clock; register CSV export. Mail in, register out, dual-running starts

Read-only mailbox ingestion that never marks mail read and never moves it, an unfiled queue with a four-rung matching ladder, and forwarded-message unwrapping so a DCI/NDC forward matches on the original sender rather than the forwarder. Infrastructure and cost

You asked for a database and storage recommendation. The answer is shaped by one fact that outranks the technical comparison: this goes through council procurement, so one vendor, one INR invoice with GST, all data in India beats a marginally better component on a second invoice from a US entity.

Everything sits in one Google Cloud project in asia-south1 (Mumbai), billed by Google Cloud India Pvt Ltd — one purchase order, one line item.

Why Cloud SQL over Supabase or Neon: both are second invoices in USD from US entities with reverse-charge GST, which is a procurement problem rather than a technical one. AlloyDB is roughly ₹28,000/month — four times the price for a workload of four complaints a month. The shared-core tier carries no SLA; at this volume that is the right trade, and upgrading is a one-line change.

Vercel is dropped for the same reason (D7). The three-app split you asked for is kept — the API is a real API from day one, and the mobile app when it comes consumes it — but the web app deploys next to it instead of on a second vendor.

The one that matters most

A nightly signed register export: a CSV of every case, every milestone date and every outcome, plus a manifest listing each document with its checksum. That protects against the project ending, not just against the infrastructure failing. Any competent person with a laptop can rebuild the register from it.

And the reminder ticker gets three independent alarms, because a silently dead ticker recreates the exact pain this product exists to remove: a cloud alert if no successful run in 26 hours; an outside-the-cloud heartbeat, so a project-wide failure still reaches a phone; and an always-visible “Reminders last ran 09:02 today” banner that turns red — visible to the person most harmed, depending on no alerting infrastructure at all.

07

Retiring the physical register

This is the single most likely way this project harms you, so it is designed rather than left to drift.

The failure mode: you enter the ten open cases, stop writing in the book by week two, then hit a bug or an outage in month three — and have neither a working system nor a maintained register, for a quasi-judicial body.

The paper register stays authoritative until an explicit, one-time, audited retirement action. Until then the dashboard carries a persistent banner: “Paper register still authoritative — day 41 of 90.” Four conditions must all be true before that action is even available:

  1. 90 consecutive days of dual-running, from the start of Phase 2.
  2. 90 consecutive verified nightly exports, checked automatically.
  3. You have personally executed the restore runbook end to end and recorded how long it took. A runbook never run by the person who will need it is not a runbook.
  4. Four consecutive clean weekly reconciliations — the screen prints that week's software register beside blank tick columns, you tick against the book, and each one is recorded.

On backfilling the ten open cases: no special mode, no skipped guards. Cases are entered normally, flagged as backfilled, with a mandatory source on every date and a photograph of the register page attached. Serial numbers are seeded from the book and new cases start after the highest — renumbering a legal register is worse than a gap.

09

Risks

Phase 1 slips and you start entering real cases into a half-built system before the dual-running discipline exists — the classic way a register migration destroys evidence.

The system refuses to create a real (non-demo) case until the four authorisation artefacts exist, and the reconciliation banner appears the moment the first real case is created, not at some later “go-live”. Build and demo against a synthetic council.

You stop logging inbound replies — which is the whole premise of the project, since things get forgotten — so reminders escalate against parties who did in fact respond.

Notice counts never move on a timer; reminders say “no reply logged”, not “did not respond”; notices 2 and 3 show you the correspondence log first. Phase 2's mailbox reader closes the gap structurally, which is exactly why it is scheduled second rather than deferred.

The mail host turns out to offer POP3 only, or refuse app passwords — invalidating Phase 2's design after it has been scheduled.

One phone call in Phase 0, with the answer written down. The preferred fallback is a forwarding rule into a council-owned mailbox: easier to get approved, and it cannot lock anyone out of registrar@.

Committee members never open the case-sheet link, so half of Phase 4 is dead weight and you keep photocopying.

Phase 4 ships the bundle PDF first — that retires the photocopier regardless of what members do — and the link is instrumented. Two or more active members across three sittings is the recorded gate for any further member investment. Failing it costs nothing already spent.

Members browsing every complaint — your explicit requirement — widens exposure: three external dentists can reach every patient's documents, not only the cases they are hearing.

Complainant contact details masked, no download and no offline caching, every view logged with a visible banner, access ending at case closure and at term end, and a confidentiality undertaking at first login. The setting defaults to your requirement, so it can be narrowed after a word with the Registrar rather than argued about now.

One person holds every account, every runbook and every alert channel — and is also the only user.

A second GitHub owner and a sealed break-glass envelope with the Registrar; a named second recipient on the three alerts that matter; a handover runbook written for a competent stranger; and the nightly export as the artefact anyone can rebuild from.

Procurement rejects cloud spend, or the council cannot open a billing account under its own PAN/GST.

Phase 0 hands the Registrar a one-page cost sheet before any infrastructure exists. One Indian vendor, one invoice; an austerity variant halves the bill; and the nightly export means the register survives a project that has to move hosts.

10

Open questions

None of these block starting Phase 1. Each names what it does block.

  • Q1Mail host — blocks Phase 2 planning. Does ksdc.in offer IMAP over TLS with an app password? Or, easier to approve: can IT set a forwarding rule from the Complaints folder into a new council-owned mailbox? One call settles a two-day task versus a redesign.
  • Q2Domain and billing — blocks Phase 0 sign-off. Can the council issue complaints.ksdc.in with DNS records, and open a Google Cloud billing account under its own PAN/GST? If cloud spend is impossible we need to know before any infrastructure exists.
  • Q3Serial numbers for the ten backlog cases. We recommend seeding from the physical book and starting new cases after the highest, so the software's numbers match the book's forever. Starting at 1 with a cross-reference column leaves two numbering systems in circulation. Please confirm.
  • Q4The ex parte rule. Is “three notices, then proceed ex parte” written anywhere — the Dentists Act, NDC regulations, the council's rules of procedure? And must the third notice go by registered post with acknowledgement due? It is built as an override-able warning either way, but the seed value should match reality.
  • Q5What does the Registrar actually wet-sign? We have assumed the GDCRI referral only. Do cease-and-desist notices, suspension orders and RTI reply covers also go out under his signature? Each one adds a print-and-scan loop.
  • Q6Committee procedure — needed before Phase 4. Must the President personally preside for a decision to be valid? Is the majority one of those present, or of the full committee of four? And does the committee record a formal division, or simply a consensus you minute?
  • Q7Retention. Does any rule prescribe how long ethics-committee proceedings must be kept? Our default is permanent retention with eight years of backups, pending a one-page schedule signed by the Registrar.
  • Q8Cross-border AI processing — gates Phase 6 only. There is no India-resident inference region today. The choice is: enable AI with a disclosure in the DPDP notice, or run a fully manual — still very useful — system. This needs an explicit yes from the Registrar before anything is built, not after.
